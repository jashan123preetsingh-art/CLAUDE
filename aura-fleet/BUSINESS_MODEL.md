# Aura Fleet — Full Business Model

*Commercial CNG cab fleet · Ludhiana & Chandigarh tricity, Punjab*
*Goal: build Punjab's largest fleet agency, one profitable car at a time.*

This document lays out the complete model we've worked through — the vision, the legal and financial structure, how the cars earn, how drivers are paid, the real numbers, the risks, and the honest caveats. Every figure here is an estimate to be confirmed on the ground; where a number is optimistic, it's flagged.

---

## 1. The vision

Own income-producing assets (cars), not a job. You don't drive; drivers operate the cars and you collect recurring cash flow. Start with 3 cars, prove the cash flow, reinvest profits into cars 4 and 5, and compound up to 10 → 20 → 50 → 100+ vehicles. Over time the business runs on managers and systems while you focus on financing, corporate contracts and scaling.

The edge is **discipline**: small start, honest numbers, tight cost control, and a driver model that shares upside so drivers stay.

---

## 2. Legal structure — LLP

The vehicles are owned by a **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)**, not by you personally.

Why LLP:
- **Limited liability** — an accident or legal claim beyond insurance hits the LLP, not your house.
- **Input Tax Credit (ITC)** — cars bought on the LLP GSTIN, used for passenger transport, unlock GST credit others can't claim (see §5).
- **Separate identity & credibility** — banks and corporate clients take a registered entity seriously.
- **Low compliance** — cheaper and lighter than a Private Limited (no heavy audit burden).

**Requirement:** the car invoice, RC, loan and insurance must all be in the **LLP's name with its GSTIN**. A car in a personal name gets zero ITC. Confirm the dealer will issue a proper tax invoice to the LLP before booking.

Before any driver keeps a rupee, each car costs the company roughly:

|---|---|

| Insurance + servicing + tyres + battery provisions | ~₹4,000–6,500 |

This ~₹20,000 is the single most important number. Every pricing decision, and especially the company floor (§8), must be measured against it.

## 5. GST & ITC

**On the car purchase — your ITC edge.** Since Sept 2025, small CNG cars attract 18% GST. On a ₹8.2L car, the embedded GST is ~₹90,000. As an LLP running passenger transport, you can **claim that ~₹90k credit per car** (plus ITC on insurance, servicing, repairs). Across 3 cars that's ~₹2.7 lakh recovered.

**But you must charge output GST to claim it:**
- **Driver dry-lease / fixed rent** → **18% GST** on the rent (you absorb it; drivers don't reclaim it).
- **Corporate contracts** → choose **5% (with driver, no car ITC)** or **12% (with ITC)**. Use the 12%-with-ITC route on corporate contracts to unlock the car credit.
- **Uber/Ola rides** → the platform handles 5% under Section 9(5); you don't file it, and you can't claim car ITC on those rides.

**Platform bills:**
- Ride fare: **5%**, collected and paid by Uber/Ola (not your cost, not your filing).
- Platform commission/service fee: **18%** — and in the commission model this is largely **non-creditable**, so it's a real sunk cost.
- Subscription apps: **18% on the subscription fee only**, no GST on the fare.

Depreciation note: if you claim ITC, income-tax depreciation runs on the ex-GST cost; commercial cars on hire get **30% WDV** (a strong early tax shield). Company tax ~25%.

## 6. Platform strategy — subscription, not commission

You register the **LLP as a fleet operator** on Ola and Uber (free, multi-car, no limit). As of late 2025, **all three platforms (Uber, Ola, Rapido) offer a subscription model for cars**, not just autos — you pay a small fixed subscription instead of ~22% per-ride commission.

Why this matters: a subscription (~₹100–150/day per car) is far cheaper than commission (~₹20,000/month on a busy car). This repairs the biggest hole that sinks most fleet plans.

**Two things to confirm at the local partner office:**
1. **Who pays the subscription and where the fare lands.** Under subscription, fares usually settle to the *driver*, not the LLP account. To take a revenue share you need to *see* the gross — so either route money through the LLP account (fleet dashboard) or rely on GPS + app dashboard verification.
2. **Aggregator licence at scale.** Under 25 cars you're fine; at 25+ you must register under the state / MoRTH aggregator guidelines.

## 7. The three driver options (your menu)

You offer every driver a choice. This is how you sign all kinds of drivers and grow fastest.

### Option A — 60/40 revenue share (default)
- Driver keeps **60%** of gross fares; company takes **40%**.
- Driver pays his **own CNG**; company pays EMI, insurance, servicing, permits, financing, subscription.
- **Company floor:** the company's share is never less than the floor (see §8).
- No guarantee to the driver — his income is his hustle, which keeps him motivated.

### Option B — Fixed rent ₹1,000/day
- For a driver who won't share earnings.
- Driver keeps **100%** of fares, pays you a flat **₹1,000/day** and his own CNG.
- Dead simple; you don't track his earnings.

### Option C — Fixed ₹26,000/month (3-month+)
- A committed monthly lease for serious drivers.
- Same ~₹1,000/day economics, but locked for 3 months or more — better stability and lower churn.

**Company net per car (after ~₹20k fixed cost), on a busy car:**
- 60/40: ~₹7,000 (busy) — earns most on a high-gross car
- Fixed ₹1,000/day: ~₹5,000–6,000
- Fixed ₹26k/month: ~₹6,000

## 8. The company floor — the rule that makes scaling safe

In the 60/40 model, the company's 40% could fall too low on a weak car. So the company takes **max(40% of gross, floor)**. The driver's 60% absorbs any shortfall.

**Critical:** the floor must equal your **true fixed cost (~₹20,000)**, not ₹15,000.

- At a **₹15k floor**, a slow car (₹32–40k gross) still loses you ~₹5,000, because ₹15k doesn't cover your ₹20k cost.
- At a **₹20k floor**, the rule becomes clean and powerful: *the driver must cover the car's fixed cost before he keeps his 60%.* A weak car can never bleed you, and only good drivers stick around (a strong driver never hits the floor).

This is what makes a large fleet safe: no single underperforming car costs you money.

## 9. CNG economics

Real operating fuel cost, from your ground data: **~₹5/km** (₹750 a tank for ~150 km in Ludhiana).

Real-world commercial mileage (loaded, city stop-go — ARAI lab figures are higher):

| Vehicle | City AC | City non-AC | Highway | ₹/km (Ludhiana, AC) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dzire Tour S CNG | ~18 km/kg | ~21 | ~24–27 | ~₹4.4 |
| Ertiga CNG | ~14 km/kg | ~16 | ~18–20 | ~₹5.7 |

- CNG price: **Ludhiana ₹84.25/kg**, **Mohali/tricity ₹97.5/kg** — Mohali drivers face ~₹13/kg more, so price cars there a touch softer.
- AC cuts mileage ~12–18%; highway improves it.
- A busy car runs ~180–200 km/day → **~₹22–25k/month CNG**, all borne by the driver in Options A and B.

**Fuel is the swing factor.** In the 60/40 model, ₹4,000 of extra CNG moves the driver's take-home by ₹4,000 — more than a 5-point change in the split.

## 10. Driver earnings (what he actually takes home)

A full **12–13 hour day, 26–27 days (290–300 hrs/month)** grosses:

| Day length | Ludhiana gross/day | Chandigarh/tricity |
|---|---|---|
| 8 hr | ₹1,300–1,700 | ₹1,400–1,900 |
| 10 hr | ₹1,700–2,300 | ₹1,900–2,500 |
| 12 hr | ₹2,100–2,900 | ₹2,400–3,200 |

- **₹65–70k/month gross** = ~₹2,500–2,700/day = a solid full-day driver.
- On **60/40** with his own CNG, take-home lands roughly **₹14,000–19,500/month** depending on gross and fuel. He reaches ₹20k+ only on a busy ₹2,700+/day car with efficient fuel.
- Chandigarh pays ~₹6–7k/month more than Ludhiana at the same hours (airport, tourism, affluent riders).

**Honest note:** you cannot *guarantee* a driver ₹25k on rent or split alone — his income is his hours. What you control (at zero cost): feed him corporate rides, keep him on all three apps, and never let the car sit idle.

## 11. Corporate contracts (the profit multiplier)

Companies pay for reliable employee/manager transport. Two shapes:

- **Light / manager commute** (morning + evening, ~2–3 hrs): bill **₹18,000–25,000/month**. The driver folds it into his day and still runs apps. You keep the billing; his reward is a small rent cut or it feeds his gross.
- **Dedicated car** (manager 9–10 hrs): bill **₹45,000–65,000/month**. Here the driver is salaried (~₹22–26k), no apps.

**Honest caveat — don't underprice corporate.** A dedicated car costs you driver + fuel + EMI + upkeep (~₹52k). A ₹55–57k bill barely breaks even; **sign at ₹62–65k+**. Bill corporate at **12%-with-ITC** to unlock the car credit, and keep the contract **in the LLP's name** so the client can't walk with the driver.

Target sectors: Ludhiana Focal Point & hosiery/auto-parts factories (night-shift worker pickup), Mohali IT City / Quark City, Chandigarh airport transfers, PGIMER and private hospitals, tricity colleges, hotels, Amritsar tour operators, and GeM government contracts.

## 12. Risk controls (investor-grade)

- **Centralized banking** — payouts route through the LLP account first, then to drivers. Never driver-direct.
- **AIS-140 GPS + immobilizer** — live tracking, geofence alerts if a car leaves the zone or idles too long; remote engine cut-off if needed.
- **Security deposit / escrow** — ₹10,000–25,000 per driver (can be built ₹1,000/week from payouts), forfeitable for damage, unpaid dues, or absconding.
- **Mandatory KYC + police verification** — commercial badge DL, Aadhaar, PAN, local police verification before the key is handed over.

## 13. Location strategy

- **Ludhiana** — industrial hub ("Manchester of India"): huge night-shift factory demand (Focal Point, hosiery, auto parts). Driver sourcing at the bus stand, Clock Tower stands, gurdwara boards, and villages around Khanna/Doraha/Kharar.
- **Chandigarh tricity** (Chandigarh–Mohali–Panchkula) — higher fares, airport (in Mohali), IT City, PGIMER, universities, tourism. Sourcing at ISBT-17/43.

Punjab-specific tailwinds: strong wedding-season demand (premium rates Nov–Feb), NRI/affluent base, corporate density. Note Chandigarh is a UT with its own RTO — watch permit/road-tax differences between Punjab and Chandigarh registrations.

1. **3 cars** — prove cash flow, refine the model, build driver pipeline.
2. Reinvest profit + reserve → **cars 4 and 5.**
3. **10 cars** — hire a supervisor, formalize collections and maintenance.

Each car adds earning capacity; profit compounds when reinvested rather than withdrawn.

## 15. Honest financial picture

On realistic inputs (Dzire, ~₹68k gross/car, real CNG, ₹20k fixed cost):

- **Company net per car:** ~₹5,000–9,000/month depending on model and gross.
- **3-car fleet:** roughly **₹15,000–27,000/month** net, before income tax.
- This is a **thin-margin, volume, patient** business. Real money starts at 10–20+ cars.
- **A maintenance reserve is survival, not optional** — at 3 cars, one major repair or two idle weeks wipes a month's profit.

Corporate contracts (properly priced) and the subscription-cost saving are what lift this from break-even to genuinely profitable.

## 16. Key risks & caveats (read before pitching)

- **Platform policy** — confirm subscription availability and money-flow for *cars* in your city; it's the linchpin.
- **EMI reality** — commercial loan rates are higher than personal; model ₹12,700, not ₹11,200.
- **CNG burn** — a busy car's fuel is ₹22–25k, not ₹16–18k; it decides the driver's take-home.
- **The floor must cover cost** — set it at ~₹20k or a slow car still loses you money.
- **Corporate underpricing** — a dedicated car needs ₹62k+; ₹55k loses money.
- **Driver retention** — below ~₹15k take-home, good drivers leave; watch the split × gross × fuel.
- **Revenue-share needs visibility** — to split gross you must see it (LLP account + GPS).
- **Utilization, drivers, maintenance, debt** — the four things that make or break every fleet.
